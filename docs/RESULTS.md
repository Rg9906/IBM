# Reading the results

The numbers live in [results/summary.md](../results/summary.md) (generated by `scripts/run_eval.py report`) and in [results/*.json](../results). This page states, hypothesis by hypothesis, what they show. All outcomes are **in our simulator, on held-out configuration set C**, paired by seed. Protocol and hypotheses: [EVAL.md](EVAL.md). Limits that apply to every row: [DEVIATIONS.md](DEVIATIONS.md).

Summary: the protocol was followed and set C was scored once. **Most of P1 to P6 are not supported as originally worded.** The clearest wins are the Governor (P5) and QueueShield versus doing nothing on S4 (P3, partly). The sections below say where QueueShield loses.

## P1: does D3 detect localised incidents sooner than D0, D1, D2?

Not supported.

- Where it detects, D3 is as fast as the best baseline: 15 s median on S3, S4 and S5. D1 is equal or faster (10 s on S3, 15 s on S4). The paired D1 minus D3 difference is 0.0 s [0.0, 0.0], p = 0.111, so there is no detectable gap.
- D3 is ahead of D0 by a median 2.5 s [0.0, 5.0] and of D2 by 5 s [5.0, 10.0] on incidents both detect.
- Coverage matters more than the median. On S2 (gateway slowdown) D3 misses 29 of 30 incidents, the same as D0 and D1; D2 misses 3. On S7 (silent drift) D0, D1 and D3 miss all 30; only D2 detects (38 s median). There are 58 incidents that D2 detects and D3 does not.
- The model adds nothing measurable over the rules: D3 and the rules-only arm D3:R have paired difference 0.0 s. The model-only arm D3:M cannot meet any false-alert budget (15.1 false alerts per hour).

The equal false-alert budget was relaxed from 1.0 to 1.5 per simulated hour because D3 could not reach 1.0 on set B (DEVIATIONS, WP-13). The "S2/S7 mostly missed" result should be read with the S2 and S7 weaknesses of the localizer and features in mind; no cause analysis has been done.

## P2: does D3 stay within the false-alert budget on surges?

Not met. On S0 and S1 combined D3 raises 4.24 false alert groups per simulated hour against a budget of 1.5 (all scenarios combined: 1.91). D0 (1.21) is within budget; D1 (1.92), D2 (2.52) and D3:R (1.61) are over it. The budget was met on tuning set B and not held out of sample. The S1 surge gate also does not reach its 95 percent labelling criterion (DEVIATIONS WP-05).

## P3: does U3 recover faster with fewer wasted retries than U0 and U1?

Partly. S4 (retry-storm trigger), 15 paired seeds:

- U3 against U0: better on every measured metric. Recovery 99 s faster (n = 9 recovered pairs), 9,396 fewer excess retries, peak queue 1,178 lower, all p < 0.01. Six U0 runs on S4 never recovered within the horizon, which is why only 9 of 15 pairs enter the recovery comparison.
- U3 against U1 (textbook failover): equal recovery (0 s), but U3 has 3,455 **more** excess retries and a 1,079 **higher** peak queue. On S4, the failover baseline is better than U3 on these two metrics.
- U3 against U2 (disciplined clients, no detection): U3 recovers 31 s faster (p < 0.001), but U2 has far fewer excess retries (median 7 versus 4,496) and the peak-queue difference is not significant (p = 0.073). The strong honest baseline wins on retries.

S3 (bank slowdown, not in the pre-registered P3 scope but in the arena): U3 does not reduce the peak queue (9,000 for both U0 and U3) and recovers slowly (99 s); U2 is best on peak queue, retries, duplicates and unique successes there. Recovery times for U0 and U1 on S3 exclude 14 runs each that never recovered, so those medians are not comparable.

## P4: does localisation prevent harmful failover?

Not demonstrated. In S3 the textbook failover (U1) made no run worse than U0 (0 of 14 runs with a failover), and U3 applied no failover (0 of 0). The comparison is therefore uninformative: the predicted harm did not appear in this simulator. The localizer itself identifies bank-side faults in only 33 percent of early S3 windows (DEVIATIONS WP-09).

## P5: does governed verification lower the status-check peak?

Supported, with a cost. On S6 (10 seeds): naive verification peaks at 517.8 status checks per second and a queue of 230; the Governor peaks at 13.0 per second and a queue of 4. Both avoid duplicates; not verifying at all produces 1,154 duplicate commits. The cost is slower resolution: median 108 s with the Governor versus 3 s naive, and 573 payments escalated to reconciliation.

## P6: does the forecaster give positive lead time with a low false-critical rate?

Not as worded.

- Discrimination is good: AUC 0.914 [0.89, 0.936], Brier 0.117 on set C (`artifacts/results/forecaster_eval.json`).
- Collapse begins 10 to 20 s after the trigger in every stuck run, and the forecast alarm arrives a median 25 s after that, so there is **no positive lead time against collapse**. The reported lead time is to the start of the stuck-judging window: median 99.5 s, p10 55.8 s.
- False criticals: S0 0.0 per hour, but S1 7.3 and S5 about 17.5 to 18.4 per hour at the tuned and 0.5 thresholds. The 0.06 per non-stuck run budget tuned on B gave 0.156 on C.
- The wording that is safe: "given a degradation in progress, the forecaster ranks runs that will lock into collapse above those that will not". It is not an outage predictor.

## Localizer

Validated on set B only (`artifacts/results/localizer_eval.json`): S4 98 percent and S6 100 percent correct; S3 bank-side 33 percent in the first 10 to 30 s; S2 and S7 mostly `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.
